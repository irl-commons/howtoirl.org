RTMP, SRT or SRTLA?

These are the three most common ways your app sends video over the internet. Which one fits depends on how steady your connection is, and how many connections you have. Here's what each does, and how to get it into OBS.

Pick one

In short:

  • One place, a steady connection, straight to the platform: RTMP.
  • On the move with one connection: SRT, to OBS, a receiver or a cloud hub.
  • On the move with two or more connections: SRTLA. It needs a receiver.

RTMP: simple, and every big platform takes it

RTMP sends your stream over TCP. TCP delivers everything, in order. When a piece goes missing, it's sent again, and everything behind it waits. On a steady connection you never notice. On a mobile connection that loses pieces now and then, those waits can add up, and the stream stutters or drops.

Twitch, YouTube and Kick all take RTMP, or RTMPS, which is RTMP with encryption. OBS uses it to stream to them.

SRT: made for connections that lose pieces

SRT sends over UDP and handles losses itself. The receiver keeps a short buffer, the latency. When a piece goes missing, the receiver asks for it again, and the resend has until the buffer runs out to arrive. In live mode, a piece that's still missing by then is dropped, so the rest of the stream doesn't wait for it.

SRT needs something on the other end that takes SRT: OBS, a receiver or a cloud hub. The ingest pages of Twitch, YouTube and Kick don't list SRT, so a hub sits in between and streams on with RTMP.

SRTLA: SRT over several connections at once

SRTLA spreads one SRT stream over two or more connections, for example two SIMs, or a SIM and a hotspot. A receiver on the internet joins the pieces back together and passes one SRT stream on to OBS or a cloud hub. OBS and the platforms don't take SRTLA directly. How SRTLA splits a stream.

Caller and listener: who connects to whom

An SRT connection has two ends. The listener waits for a connection on a port. The caller connects to it. Your phone app is usually the caller, and whatever receives the stream listens.

  • Watch out: The listener has to be reachable from where the caller is. A PC at home is hidden behind your router, so a phone on mobile data can't reach it until you forward the port on the router (SRT uses UDP).
  • Watch out: Some internet connections share one public address among many customers (carrier-grade NAT). Then a forwarded port on your own router isn't enough: the port would also have to be opened on your provider's side, and that's up to your provider. A server or a hosted receiver avoids the problem.

Let OBS listen

OBS can take SRT in a Media Source. Untick "Local File" and enter an address with mode=listener at the end, like srt://0.0.0.0:9000?mode=listener. Here 0.0.0.0 means "on every network this PC is on", and 9000 is the port your app sends to. In your app, enter the PC's address and the same port.

OBS can also call: to take a stream from a receiver, enter the receiver's address without mode=listener.

The quickest way to try SRT. It works only while the phone is on the same network as the PC.

  1. Phone appSRT or SRTLA, calls
  2. Hosted receivera service runs it
  3. OBSat home or in the cloud, calls the receiver

No port forwarding, and no server to look after. Hosted receivers are listed on the receiver page.

  1. Phone appSRTLA, calls
  2. SRTLA receiveron a rented server, joins the connections
  3. SRT serveron the same server
  4. OBScalls the SRT server
  5. PlatformRTMP

Free software on a server you rent. Choosing SRT latency

Latency is how long the receiver waits for late and resent pieces. More latency means more delay before your viewers see you, and fewer glitches when the connection stumbles.

  • SRT's own default is 120 ms. Both ends can set a latency, and the higher of the two is used.
  • The rule of thumb from Haivision, who made SRT: latency is the round-trip time times a multiplier. On a good network with little loss, about 4 times the round trip. With more loss, more: 8 times at up to 10 % loss. It should also be longer than the longest burst of loss you expect.
  • On the move, losses can come in bursts, for example while the phone switches towers. That's likely why phone apps start much higher than the rule gives for a ping: Moblin's default is 3000 ms (3 seconds). BELABOX starts at 2000 ms and recommends 1500 to 2500 ms.
  • Watch out: In an SRT address in OBS, latency is in microseconds, not milliseconds: 2 seconds is latency=2000000. Without it, OBS asks for SRT's 120 ms, and the stream uses the other end's setting if that's higher. Moblin and BELABOX count in milliseconds.
