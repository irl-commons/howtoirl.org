Find what broke while you're live

Your stream passes through several parts on its way to your viewers. When something goes wrong, one of them stopped. Check them in order, from the camera to the platform, and you find it faster than by changing settings at random.

Follow the stream from the camera to your viewers

In short: find the last part where everything still looks right. The part after it is the one to fix.

  1. Camerathe phone's own, or one on a cable or Wi-Fi
  2. Your appon the phone, or a backpack's encoder
  3. Networksmobile, Wi-Fi or satellite
  4. Receiverjoins bonded connections back together
  5. HubOBS at home or in the cloud: your scenes
  6. PlatformTwitch, YouTube, Kick

Not every setup has every part. A phone that streams straight to the platform has no receiver and no hub. Bonding needs a receiver. Scenes like BRB need a hub.

Where to look at each part

  • Camera: the preview in your app. If it moves there, the camera and its link to the phone are fine.
  • Your app: the bitrate it sends. When it bonds, each connection's traffic too. Moblin shows upload statistics per connection, and BELABOX lists each connection's throughput and the total.
  • Receiver: most have a statistics page that shows whether a stream arrives, and at what bitrate. A scene switcher such as NOALBS reads the same numbers.
  • Hub: the preview and the audio mixer in OBS, or the cloud service's own preview.
  • Platform: its own dashboard shows what really arrives. On Twitch that's Twitch Inspector at inspector.twitch.tv, with a graph of your bitrate as it reaches Twitch. On YouTube, the Live Control Room shows your stream's health and messages such as a bitrate lower than recommended. On Kick, the Creator Dashboard shows a preview of what Kick receives, and a Session Health badge.

Drops and freezes on the move

A short glitch while you move is normal: when your phone switches to another cell tower, the connection can lose data or stall for a moment (more on why, with sources). You can't fully avoid that. You can change how long a bad moment lasts, and what your viewers see during it.

Let the bitrate drop before the stream does

A high bitrate doesn't look better if the connection can't carry it. Then the picture freezes instead. With adaptive bitrate on, your app lowers the bitrate when the connection gets weak and raises it again when it recovers. Moblin and BELABOX both have it. VDO.Ninja's IRL guide says the same: if it still freezes while you move, try a lower profile before you raise the bitrate. Smaller frames recover faster after a loss.

Give the stream time to recover

With SRT and SRTLA, the receiver waits a set time for late and resent pieces of the stream. That time is the SRT latency. Longer means more delay, but fewer glitches. How to pick it.

With SRTLA, your app stops using a connection when it has heard nothing back from the receiver on it for a few seconds: 4 in BELABOX's sender, 5 in Moblin. It keeps trying, and uses the connection again once the receiver answers.

Show your viewers something while it's down

A BRB scene doesn't stop a drop. It shows your viewers something better than a frozen picture until the feed comes back. A scene switcher does this for you. NOALBS, for example, reads your receiver's bitrate every second. It switches to a LOW scene when the bitrate falls to a limit you set, to BRB when the feed is gone, and back to LIVE when it recovers.

On Twitch, Disconnect Protection covers a drop between Twitch and whatever sends it your stream: your hub, or your app if it streams straight to Twitch. You switch it on yourself in Stream Settings, with "Temporary Backup Image" on too. If your connection to Twitch drops, viewers see a placeholder for up to 90 seconds, and the recording stays in one piece. If you don't reconnect in that time, the stream ends as normal.

  • Watch out: If your phone streams straight to the platform, viewers see no BRB scene while its connection is down, whatever the phone shows. At most they see the platform's own placeholder, if it has one. A BRB scene needs a hub between your app and the platform.
  • Watch out: After a dropout, OBS's own media source can leave the sound glitchy and out of sync until you restart the source. NOALBS's !fix chat command restarts it. A source made for IRL such as OBS IRL Source tries to reconnect every 2 seconds, where the media source waits 10. Its makers say the sound survives a bad connection.

Before you ask for help

A tip from the people who help in app communities: these are the questions they ask first. Have the answers ready, and you get help sooner.

  • Your app and its version, and your phone or encoder with its system version.
  • Your networks: which carriers, and whether it's a SIM, a hotspot or Wi-Fi.
  • When it happened, with your time zone, and how long it lasted.
  • The last part in the chain above where everything still looked right.
  • What you already tried.
  • A screenshot of your app's statistics and of the receiver's statistics page, if you have one.
  • Watch out: Blank out your stream key, passwords and any stream ID in an address before you share a screenshot. On many services, any of them is enough for someone else to stream as you.
