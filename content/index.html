Maybe it's time for a backpack

A backpack carries a small streaming computer, mobile modems and a battery. It takes a real camera, so your phone doesn't have to do it all.

Before you buy one: if heat and battery are the only problems, keep the phone out of the sun and try a power bank. Unplug the power bank when the phone gets hot. If the stream only drops in crowds, a second network is the smaller step.

Keep the phone cool and charged

  • Keep it out of direct sun, and don't shut it in a bag or pocket where the heat can't get out. Sources: Apple, Google, Samsung
  • A weak signal makes the phone work harder and get warmer. So a stronger network also helps with heat. Source: Samsung
  • Heat damages the battery. Very hot conditions can shorten its life permanently. Charging while it's hot makes this worse. Sources: Apple, Apple batteries
  • Charging while streaming adds heat. If it gets hot while charging, unplug it, let it cool down, then charge again. Sources: Google, Samsung
  • Lower the resolution, frame rate or bitrate in your streaming app. High-quality video and heavy uploading make a phone warm. Sources: Apple, Google
  • A newer codec, HEVC (H.265) instead of H.264, needs less data for the same picture. Your receiver and the platform must accept it (Kick takes only H.264). Choosing SRT or RTMP doesn't change the codec. Sources: YouTube, Kick

Extra cooling Work in progress

Tips from other streamers. I haven't tested them yet:

  • Small stick-on heatsinks, the kind sold for Raspberry Pi boards, on the phone's hottest spot.
  • Clip-on phone coolers.
  • Gaming phones, built with extra cooling.

Where the hot spot is depends on the phone.

One phone is enough

One phone can do a lot, and you carry nothing extra. Stay with it as long as it works.

Apps that help

  • Moblin: free and open source, for iPhone, with bonding (mobile data and Wi-Fi at once) built in.
  • IRL Pro: for Android, with bonding and a free bonding service.
  • IRL+ Cast: for Android, in open testing.
  • LifeStreamer: for Android, open source, with USB video in and bonding.
  • IRLwhatever: an all-in-one Android app, in open beta, with USB cameras, scenes, bonding with Moblink spare phones, chat and overlays.
  • TVU Go: one app that combines cellular, Wi-Fi and tethered phones, by subscription.
  • Larix Broadcaster: for Android and iOS.
  • The platforms' own apps: the simplest start, without bonding.

Choose something else when crowds, long days or heat end your streams: add a second network, or think about a backpack.

Add a second network

Add a connection on a different mobile network, with its own radio. Your streaming app then has to use both at once, which is called bonding. A hotspot on its own doesn't do that.

The second connection

  • A second phone's hotspot, on a different network than your main phone.
  • A pocket 5G hotspot with its own SIM.
  • Moblink: turns spare Android phones into extra connections; iPhones running Moblin can do the same. Free and open source, in early testing (alpha).
  • Speedify: an app that combines several internet connections for any traffic, with its own service, by subscription.

You also need an app that bonds. Moblin, IRL Pro, IRL+ Cast, LifeStreamer, IRLwhatever and TVU Go do: see the phone apps.

Test it. During a test stream, each connection should show traffic. If a connection is connected but shows zero traffic, it isn't helping yet.

Choose something else when you need many networks, or a real camera on your back: think about a backpack.

Next: bonding with SRTLA needs a receiver that joins the stream back together. Some apps and services include one. If yours doesn't, pick a receiver.

Why it helps: one network has short bad moments, even a fast one. A second network covers them. Why more than one network?

Build your own backpack

A small board, modems and a camera become a streaming backpack. You choose every part.

Systems you build yourself

  • BELABOX: an IRL encoder on small boards with modems, open source, with build guides.
  • CeraLive: "a live-streaming system you build yourself", built on open source. Its control panel, bonding sender and receivers are public on GitHub; the streaming engine on the device isn't. Not released yet. Its docs list hardware, modems and audio devices.
  • IRLbox: a ready-made encoder system for Orange Pi and Radxa boards, with SRT, RTMP and RIST. Free download in open beta; no source code or licence published.

Choose something else when it has to work right away, with support: ready-made backpacks.

Next: if your backpack bonds with SRTLA, it needs a receiver.

Move production to the cloud

The show keeps running even if your PC, power or internet at home fails.

What helps

  • IRLToolkit: cloud production with overlays and drop protection; several inputs on the bigger plan.
  • IRLhosting: cloud OBS servers for IRL streamers, with bonding and disconnect protection.
  • OBS Studio on a server you run yourself: the software is free and open source, the server isn't.
  • NOALBS: switches OBS scenes automatically when the bitrate drops; open source.

Choose something else when most of your streams are at your desk and your PC is reliable. Then you don't need this.

Next: get the stream into OBS. The same steps work for OBS on a server. Going live in your app only starts the feed to the cloud. Start the cloud's output too.

A hub, plus your phone or backpack

A hybrid stream mixes your desk or studio with streaming away from it, in another room or out of the house. You switch between them, or bring your phone into a scene at home. You need two parts: a hub, where the show is put together, and a phone or backpack that sends from wherever you are.

The hub (it doesn't have to be in the cloud)

  • OBS Studio on your own PC at home, taking your phone's stream from a relay or receiver: see receivers and into OBS.
  • Production in the cloud: IRLToolkit, IRLhosting, or OBS on a server you run: see cloud production.

Away from the desk

  • One phone: see the phone apps.
  • A phone with a second network: see a second network.
  • A backpack: build or buy one.

Choose something else when you only stream from one place. Then one of the two parts is enough.

Next: if you bond, the hub needs a receiver. Some services include one.

A receiver for your bonded stream

When a stream travels over several networks at once, a server joins it back together. Any app or backpack that uses the same protocol can use this server, with your own settings.

Hosted or your own

  • BELABOX Cloud: hosted receivers for SRTLA, as a perk for BELABOX sponsors. Other hosted services, and some apps such as IRL Pro, include one too.
  • OpenIRL srtla-receiver: run your own SRTLA receiver, open source.
  • IRL+ BOND: hosted relays for IRL+'s own bonding protocol, used from IRL+'s apps.

Also think about where the show itself runs: on your PC or in the cloud.

Into OBS

Four parts work together. Your app or box sends the stream. A receiver joins it back together. OBS takes one stream from the receiver. A scene switcher shows BRB when the feed drops.

In OBS

  • Going live in your app sends the stream to your receiver or hub. A hub goes live on the platform with its own Start, so press that too.
  • Add the receiver's address to OBS as a source, not the phone's. The receiver can be a service, a rented server or a computer at home: see receivers.
  • OBS's own media source was made for playing files. A source made for IRL, such as OBS IRL Source, recovers on its own after a dropout and keeps the audio clean.
  • A scene switcher like NOALBS shows BRB for you when the bitrate drops, and switches back when it recovers. It reads the receiver's stats address, which isn't the address OBS plays. Use one automatic switcher per OBS.

Choose something else when you'd rather not run OBS at all. A hosted hub can do the switching: see hub options.

Add a satellite dish

A dish gives you a signal where mobile networks are too busy or missing. It joins the rest as one more connection.

What helps

  • Starlink: satellite internet, as one more link for a phone setup or a backpack.

Choose something else when trees and buildings block the sky. To use it while moving, you need the right plan and mount.

Next: using it together with mobile networks is bonding. Your app or backpack has to support it, and SRTLA needs a receiver.
